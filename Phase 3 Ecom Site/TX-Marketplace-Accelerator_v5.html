Listings built to rank on Amazon AU — not just to exist

Amazon's A10 algorithm weights your listing's keywords, conversion rate, and sales velocity to decide where you rank. A listing built with US keyword data, generic bullet points, or the wrong category doesn't just rank low — it costs more in PPC to compensate for every day it's live.

We build listings using AU-specific search data. The terms Australians use are different from US search behaviour. The volumes are lower. The keyword strategy has to reflect that — or you're optimising for an audience that isn't buying on Amazon AU.

Listing optimisation isn't a one-time task. We update your listings as your sales data accumulates, as search trends shift, and as your PPC campaigns identify new high-converting terms.

What we optimise
  • Keyword research — AU-specific, ranked by volume and competition
  • Title structure — algorithm and human optimised
  • 5 bullet points — each addressing a buyer objection or search term
  • Backend search terms — full character count, no repetition
  • Category and browse node placement
  • Ongoing updates as data accumulates

Advertising that builds something, not just buys sales

Most store owners who try Amazon PPC themselves hit the same wall: spend goes up, sales don't follow, and the account gets switched off after one bad month. The campaigns weren't structured correctly from the start, the keywords were guesses, and no one is actively managing the bids.

We build PPC from a campaign architecture first. Sponsored Products for keyword targeting, Sponsored Brands for top-of-search visibility, Sponsored Display for retargeting. Each type has a role. We don't mix them or run one without the others having context.

Every week, we review bids. Every two weeks, we mine the Search Term Report. Every month, we report ACoS and TACoS — because TACoS tells you what advertising is doing to your organic performance over time. That's the number that matters.

PPC management includes
  • Campaign architecture (Sponsored Products, Brands, Display)
  • AU-specific keyword research and match type strategy
  • Weekly bid management and placement adjustments
  • Bi-weekly search term report analysis
  • Negative keyword management
  • Monthly ACoS and TACoS reporting

Ad spend is your budget, billed directly by Amazon. Not included in the monthly management fee. Recommended starting budget: $500–$1,500/month for new sellers with 5–10 SKUs.

FBA running without surprises

FBA is almost always the right choice for AU brands — Prime badge, Buy Box advantage, Amazon-handled customer service and returns. But FBA has fees, and those fees need to be modelled before you send a single unit.

An item that costs $4 to ship yourself might cost $6.50 in FBA fees — but if FBA lifts your conversion rate enough to compensate, the net is positive. We run the calculation before we configure anything.

Once FBA is live, we monitor inventory weekly against your sales velocity, manage storage fees before they accumulate, and audit for reimbursements quarterly. Amazon occasionally loses or damages FBA stock. You're entitled to be paid for it — but Amazon doesn't always process it automatically.

FBA management includes
  • FBA vs FBM margin modelling for your catalogue
  • FBA shipment plan creation and label generation
  • Packing and prep instructions
  • Weekly inventory level monitoring
  • Storage fee management and removal orders
  • FBA reimbursement audits (quarterly)
  • Stranded inventory monitoring and resolution

The closest thing Amazon gives you to a landing page

A+ Content is the enhanced product page available to Brand Registry holders. It replaces the plain-text description field with image-and-copy modules — comparison tables, feature callouts, lifestyle rows, brand story sections.

Most store owners think their DTC brand story translates directly to Amazon. It doesn't. Amazon buyers scan. They compare. They look at images first, bullet points second, A+ Content last — when they're almost sold and looking for a reason to close the deal or walk away.

We write A+ Content around the buyer's remaining objections, not your brand's preferred narrative. Then we brief the design so it gets executed without guesswork.

A+ Content includes
  • Competitor audit and gap analysis
  • Conversion-focused copy for all modules
  • Module structure and sequence recommendations
  • Creative brief for design execution
Brand Storefront includes

  • Copy for all pages

Brand Registry required (registered trademark). We advise on the process if you don't have one yet — typically 6–9 months in Australia.

Amazon working. Now let's expand.

40% of Australian online sales happen on marketplaces. Amazon is the largest — but eBay AU, Catch, Bunnings Marketplace, TheIconic, and Woolworths Everyday Market collectively represent tens of millions of additional monthly buyers.

We don't recommend launching across multiple marketplaces simultaneously. Each has its own algorithm, fee structure, buyer demographic, and listing requirements. Get Amazon working first. The catalogue is built. The copy is done. Expansion to eBay and Catch is an adaptation, not a rebuild.
