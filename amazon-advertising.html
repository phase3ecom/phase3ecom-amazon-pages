Why it fails

Why Amazon PPC burns money when you run it yourself

If you've tried Amazon PPC and stopped, you're not alone. Most Shopify brands that attempt it without expertise hit the same wall: spend goes up, sales don't follow, and the account gets switched off after one bad month.

🏗

Wrong campaign structure from the start

Amazon has three Sponsored ad types — Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display — each with different targeting methods, bidding logic, and objectives. Running them without a campaign architecture means you're competing with yourself.

🎯

Keyword strategy built on guesses

Without AU-specific search volume data and match type strategy, you bid on terms that are too broad, too expensive, or attracting the wrong buyer. US keyword data underperforms in Australia — the market is different.

📉

Bids aren't managed

Amazon PPC rewards active management. A campaign set up and left to run drifts toward poor-performing placements and inflated CPCs within weeks. Most sellers set and forget — and wonder why it stops working.

📊

TACoS isn't being tracked

Most sellers watch ACoS. The better metric is TACoS — total advertising cost of sale, including organic revenue. A high ACoS on a product with strong organic lift is often profitable. Without this visibility, the wrong campaigns get cut.

How we run it

How we run Amazon Advertising

Campaign architecture first

Before we spend a dollar, we build a campaign structure that separates ad types, targets, and match types into logical groupings. Sponsored Products for high-intent keyword targeting. Sponsored Brands for top-of-search visibility. Sponsored Display for retargeting and competitor conquest. Each has a role. We don't mix them.

AU-specific keyword strategy

We build keyword targets from Amazon AU search data — not US or UK data. We layer broad, phrase, and exact match types systematically, with separate campaigns for each so bid management is clean and conversion data is usable.

Ongoing bid management

Weekly bid reviews based on actual conversion data. We adjust by keyword, placement, and time of day. We don't set and forget — because Amazon's algorithm doesn't either. Campaigns that aren't actively managed drift toward inefficiency within 2–3 weeks.

Search Term Report analysis

Every two weeks, we mine the Search Term Report to identify new high-converting terms to add and irrelevant terms to negative-match out. This is what separates managed campaigns from self-managed ones — it's the work most sellers never do.

TACoS reporting

We report on ACoS and TACoS. You see the full picture — not just what advertising costs, but what it's contributing to organic performance over time. This is the metric that tells you whether your ad spend is building something or just buying sales.
